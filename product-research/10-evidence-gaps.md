# 10: Evidence Gaps, Limitations, and Rejected Claims

## Major Unanswered Questions
- GAP-001: Behavioral Elasticity Heterogeneity: While aggregate marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is well documented (~0.6 to 0.8), empirical data shows wide divergence across wealth deciles (low-wealth households have MPC approaching 0.95, while high-wealth households have MPC below 0.3). How should this distribution be parameterized in a simplified 6-sector aggregate model?
  - Required Resolution: Benchmark against Federal Reserve Survey of Consumer Finances (SCF) distribution parameters in a future iteration.
- GAP-002: Cross-Border Foreign Exchange & Balance of Payments: The current model focuses on a consolidated sovereign financial system (closed economy). How will the inclusion of an external sector (foreign exchange rates, trade deficits, foreign central bank reserves) impact the internal balance sheet conservation matrix?
  - Required Resolution: Design the sector matrix with an extensible foreign sector column for phase two expansion.

## Weakly Evidenced Requirements
- GAP-003: Cognitive Overload Thresholds for Multi-Sector Graphs: While cognitive load literature demonstrates that minimal typography and whitespace improve comprehension, empirical tests on the exact maximum number of simultaneous animated SVG particle conduits before visual distraction occurs are limited.
  - Required Resolution: Conduct A/B testing on particle flow density with student focus groups.

## Inaccessible Product Flows or Paid Data
- Proprietary Institutional Risk Systems: Proprietary enterprise risk management systems (e.g. BlackRock Aladdin, MSCI RiskMetrics) have inaccessible proprietary source code and closed institutional licenses. However, publicly available academic risk models (Value at Risk, Expected Shortfall, Monte Carlo Brownian motion) provide sufficient empirical validity for educational modeling.

## Rejected Claims and Reasons
- REJ-001: "Central banks can set both the money supply quantity and the interest rate simultaneously."
  - Reason for Rejection: Contradicted by central bank operating procedures (EVD-CB-001). Central banks can set the price of reserves (the policy rate) or the quantity of reserves, but the downward-sloping demand for reserves means they cannot independently fix both without creating arbitrage or liquidity shortages.
- REJ-002: "Bank reserves are lent out to non-bank borrowers in the real economy."
  - Reason for Rejection: Contradicted by empirical central banking literature (EVD-BNK-001). Bank reserves exist only on the liability side of the central bank balance sheet and can only circulate between commercial banks and the central bank; commercial banks lend by creating new customer deposit accounts.
- REJ-003: "High sovereign debt causes immediate hyperinflation regardless of productive capacity."
  - Reason for Rejection: Contradicted by decades of empirical data from Japan and post-2008 developed markets where high sovereign debt-to-GDP coexisted with low inflation or deflation due to private sector savings preferences and output gaps.
